The Problem

Small law firms are the backbone of Ontario's legal market. Sole practitioners make up 17–20% of practicing lawyers; add firms of 2–10 lawyers and you're at roughly 50–60% of private practice.

These firms face the same pressure from AI as Bay Street. Clients expect faster turnaround, and opposing counsel is already using AI to prepare.

Legal AI vendors like Harvey are built for large firms and big budgets. Small firms are largely on their own without an in-house IT department to explain, in plain terms, what their AI options actually are.

Worse, faced with the pressure of the time crunch, a lawyer might even take the extraordinary step of entering case data into an LLM without knowing what happens to that data. Or they may use an LLM to generate text without knowing for sure if the output contains hallucinations.

The reputational risks of using AI are real, as are the time-saving benefits.

The question is: which tools are worth the time and cost? And how to use these in a way that is always on the right side of professional liability?

My Proposition — AI can give you back your time

I specialize in helping small law firms become AI-enabled.

Done right, adopting AI helps a small firm gain their time back. These are the hours spent on generating leads for new clients, hours spent doing client intake and drafting responses. Every non-billable hour saved is time you get back.

AI can also help reduce time spent on manual tasks like identifying key issues across hundreds of discovery case files, and reviewing a corpus of internal case files to identify precedents. AI can help you reduce review time and give you more time for strategic thinking.

I can help you to understand:

  • What AI can (and cannot do) for you and your workflow
  • What your best AI options are given your current tech stack
  • What the different reputational risks are for different AI options, and recommendations to guard against those risks

The Process

  2. AI Tooling Assessment
